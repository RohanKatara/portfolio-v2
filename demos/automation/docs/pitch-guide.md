# Three demos to use in a cold approach

These are clearly labelled interactive simulations with fictional records. The calculations and approval decisions work; extraction and message interpretation are preset. Do not describe them as a connected live deployment or claim measured savings.

## A three-minute walkthrough

### 1. Enquiry to quotation
Open the Quotations page and choose Standard enquiry.
Say: "Here is the kind of enquiry your sales desk receives. The workflow organises the requirements, checks the catalogue, and puts a quote in front of a person."
Run scenario, review the two items and ₹14,372.40 total, then approve.
Show the assigned salesperson, printable quotation and dated next action. Change the owner if useful.
Click Advance sample date to 29 Sep: the quotation follow-up becomes overdue. Record the sample follow-up as reviewed; the next action changes to checking the customer's decision. Choose Customer accepts quotation or Customer declines quotation and show that follow-up stops.
Switch to Unclear specification. Run it. Show that approval is blocked until Single phase or Three phase is selected.
Ask: "Which details does your team most often have to chase before quoting?"

### 2. Purchase-order processing
Open Purchase orders and choose Unfamiliar item code.
Say: "This is a customer ordering from you. Their code AE-BRG04 needs translating into your catalogue. Their price also differs from the agreed price book."
Run scenario. Show the two blocked checks, then confirm the mapping from the preset customer reply. One blocked check remains.
Choose Customer corrects to ₹320, then create the sales-order draft. Download the sample CSV and show that the reviewed quantities, SKU and price are carried into it. It is a generic handoff file, not a configured ERP import.
Click Check this PO again to demonstrate the duplicate block.
Ask: "How are prices, product codes, and duplicate POs checked today?"

### 3. Payment follow-up
Open Receivables and reset if necessary.
Run the first invoice. Choose Customer says paid and apply the next step.
Say: "A payment claim does not automatically settle an invoice. The balance stays open until receipt is confirmed."
Click Confirm sample payment received and show the reduced outstanding total.
Reset, then show Quantity dispute: the task is assigned to Arjun, reminders pause and the invoice stays outstanding. Record resolved delivery dispute using the preset evidence. Responsibility returns to accounts and payment follow-up resumes; the balance is unchanged.

Two useful alternatives:
- Invoice copy needed: reminders pause; mark the sample invoice copy provided, then show the next check scheduled two days later. No real message or document is sent.
- Promise to pay: apply the 30 Sep promise, then advance the sample clock to 1 Oct. The missed promise returns to the reminder queue. Confirmed paid invoices never return.
Ask: "Who tracks payment promises and disputes, and where does that information live?"

## A short portfolio description

Interactive workflow prototypes for industrial businesses: enquiries with assigned follow-up, customer POs converted into checked sales-order drafts, and overdue invoices with tracked blockers and due actions. Explore preset examples, resolve exceptions and inspect the resulting records. Fictional sample data; no live AI or external systems are connected.

## Positioning the next step

Offer to map one specific process using anonymised examples from the business. A connected pilot would require their actual rules, a clean product/customer reference, integration access, and success measures agreed in advance.

Useful baseline measures:
- Enquiries or purchase orders per month.
- Minutes spent on each document.
- The most common missing fields or corrections.
- Who approves a price exception.
- How payment confirmations reach the accounts team.
